Capabilities

Any limb. Any movement. Any art.

Built for people who can't hold a brush — but can move something.

Head & face tracking

Nod, tilt, and turn your head to steer the plotter across the full canvas. Full 2D drawing from neck movement alone, using MediaPipe's 478-point face mesh running at 30fps in your browser.

Hand & wrist

Wave a hand in the camera frame — the wrist landmark drives the pen. Fine gestures make fine strokes.

Any bright object

A foot, elbow, or anything that contrasts with the background can become your drawing cursor.

Repurposed 3D printer as plotter

We strip the extruder from a discarded 3D printer and strap a pen where it was. Your body's X/Y position streams over USB as G-code, driving the same stepper motors that used to push plastic — now pushing ink.

Private by design

All vision runs locally in your browser via WebAssembly. Zero video is ever sent to a server.

Process

From discarded printer to art machine in minutes.

01

Rescue a 3D printer

Find any discarded or broken FDM printer. Remove the extruder and hotend — the frame, rails, and stepper motors are all you need.

02

Attach a pen

Mount a pen or marker where the extruder was. Load paper onto the bed. Your old printer is now a plotter.

03

Connect & point the camera

Plug the printer in over USB and open Plotter in your browser. Aim the webcam at whatever limb someone can move — head, hand, foot, or elbow.

04

Move. The printer draws.

Body position streams as G-code over Web Serial in real time. The same steppers that used to push plastic now push ink — your movement becomes art on paper.

Plotter started with a simple but powerful question: what if someone can't hold a brush? For people with limited motor ability — whether from a condition, an injury, or a disability — traditional art tools are inaccessible. The canvas stays blank not because of a lack of creativity, but because of a lack of tools.

The idea grew out of Ramsey Mussalum's Design for Social Good course at Sonoma Academy — a class built around one idea: design should solve real problems for real people.

And there was a second problem hiding in plain sight: millions of 3D printers are discarded every year — broken, outdated, or simply abandoned. Those machines still have functional frames, rails, and stepper motors. Instead of the landfill, Plotter gives them a new purpose. Strip the extruder, strap a pen in its place, and the same hardware that once built plastic objects now creates art on paper.

Point a webcam at whatever limb someone can move — a head, a wrist, a foot — and body position streams over USB as G-code. The printer's X and Y axes follow in real time. The person becomes the artist. The machine becomes their hand.

Rescued hardware. Zero subscriptions. Just movement, and art.
