How it works.

1

Open the app

Use the browser demo above or download the desktop app and connect your plotter over USB.

2

Point the camera

Aim the webcam at whichever body part the person can move — head, hand, foot, or elbow.

3

Start drawing

Move freely. The tracker follows the target point and translates it into X/Y coordinates.

4

Plotter creates art

The physical plotter moves its pen arm to mirror your movements — producing real artwork on paper.

The Story

Art should have no barriers.

Plotter started with a simple but powerful question: what if someone can't hold a brush? For people with limited motor ability — whether from a condition, an injury, or a disability — traditional art tools are inaccessible. The canvas stays blank not because of a lack of creativity, but because of a lack of tools.

The idea grew out of work at Sonoma Academy, in Ramsey Mussalum's Design for Social Good course — a class built around one idea: design should solve real problems for real people.

Plotter's answer is to move the paintbrush entirely off the person's body. You point a webcam at whatever limb someone can move — a head, a wrist, a foot — and a physical pen plotter mimics that movement on paper. The person becomes the artist; the machine becomes their hand.

No expensive hardware. No proprietary software. Just a webcam, a plotter, and the freedom to create.
