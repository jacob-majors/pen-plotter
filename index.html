Capabilities

Any limb.
Any movement. Any art.

Built for people who can't hold a brush — but can move something.

Head & face tracking

Nod, tilt, and turn your head to steer the plotter across the full canvas. Full 2D drawing from neck movement alone using MediaPipe's 478-point face mesh.

Hand & wrist

Wave a hand in frame — the wrist landmark drives the pen. Fine gestures make fine lines.

Any body part

Foot, elbow, shoulder — in "bright object" mode, anything that contrasts with the background becomes the drawing cursor.

Physical plotter output

Connect a pen plotter or CNC over serial. Live X/Y coordinates stream from the browser to the machine — your body becomes the arm.

Private by design

All vision runs locally in your browser via WebAssembly. Zero video is ever sent to a server.

Plotter started with a simple but powerful question: what if someone can't hold a brush? For people with limited motor ability — whether from a condition, an injury, or a disability — traditional art tools are inaccessible. The canvas stays blank not because of a lack of creativity, but because of a lack of tools.

The idea grew out of Ramsey Mussalum's Design for Social Good course at Sonoma Academy — a class built around one idea: design should solve real problems for real people.

Plotter moves the paintbrush entirely off the person's body. Point a webcam at whatever limb someone can move — a head, a wrist, a foot — and a physical pen plotter mimics those movements on paper. The person becomes the artist. The machine becomes their hand.

No expensive hardware. No subscriptions. Just movement and art.
